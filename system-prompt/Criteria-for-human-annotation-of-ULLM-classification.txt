We define an uncensored large language model (ULLM) as a model that lacks proper content filtering protections and ethical safeguards, and is therefore capable of generating harmful or unrestricted content. In this study, annotators determine whether a model satisfies this definition based solely on information explicitly provided in its model metadata (e.g., model card or README), without performing any external validation, model execution, or inference beyond the given text.

A model is labeled as a ULLM if and only if there exists clear and explicit evidence in the metadata indicating at least one of the following conditions: (1) the model is explicitly described as uncensored, unaligned, or equivalent; (2) the metadata states the absence, removal, or disabling of safety mechanisms, such as filtering, guardrails, or refusal behaviors; (3) the model is described as capable of generating harmful or unrestricted content (e.g., offensive text, malicious code, or NSFW content); or (4) the metadata explicitly describes the use of uncensoring-oriented development techniques, such as training on de-aligned or toxic datasets, refusal-direction ablation, removal of alignment constraints, or ULLM merging.

Annotators must base their decisions strictly on explicit statements in the metadata. They must not infer uncensored capabilities from indirect signals, vague descriptions, or general model properties. 

If the metadata contains explicit statements indicating the presence of safety mechanisms, filtering, alignment, or guardrails, or if there is insufficient or ambiguous evidence regarding uncensored behavior, the model must be labeled as non-ULLM. In cases of conflicting information, annotators prioritize explicit statements regarding safety properties and adopt a conservative strategy, assigning a positive label only when the evidence of uncensored behavior is strong and unambiguous.

Overall, annotators follow a conservative and evidence-based principle: a model is labeled as a ULLM only when its uncensored nature is explicitly and clearly supported by the metadata.
